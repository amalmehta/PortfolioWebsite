Things I'm thinking about

Open questions and ideas I keep coming back to. Updated as my thinking changes.

  1. How do we build tools that help people escape inequity?

    Some of the most useful technology lowers the cost of something that used to take money, connections or the right zip code: a tutor, a coach, a clinic, a second opinion. AI could widen that access or concentrate it further. I keep asking what it takes to build tools that reach the people who need them most: cheap enough to run, simple enough to trust, and designed with them rather than for them. And how to tell whether a tool actually changed someone’s odds, not just looked helpful.

    Added October 11, 2026

  2. What can brains teach robots about learning to move?

    Animals pick up new movements from a handful of tries, keep perceiving while they act, and correct themselves mid-motion. Most robot learning still leans on huge amounts of data and separate stages for seeing, planning and moving. Sensorimotor AI asks what changes if perception and action are learned together, as one loop. It’s the thread running through the Sensorimotor AI journal club at Berkeley’s Redwood Center and through my own work decoding hand movement from motor-cortex spikes. I want to know which of the brain’s tricks (prediction, fast feedback, learning from little) actually carry over to agents, and which are just biology.

    Added October 11, 2026

  3. Where do vision-language-action models meet physical intelligence?

    VLAs bring internet-scale pretraining to robot control: one model that sees, reads an instruction and outputs actions. What I keep wondering is how much of the hard part that covers (contact, balance, recovering when the world doesn’t match the plan) and how much still needs reinforcement learning, simulation and real-world experience. Training a robot dog in simulation that then hit its target about 60% of the time on hardware made that gap concrete for me. I’m curious where the two approaches meet: VLAs for breadth, RL for the last stretch of skill.

    Added October 11, 2026
