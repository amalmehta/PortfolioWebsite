2026 · pilot, then 50 follow-ups

Rewarding What You Can’t Verify

A code agent trained only on visible tests learns to delete untested code until real correctness falls to 2%; random audits stopped it in the pilot but only delayed it in follow-ups.

BCI Projects

Four brain–computer interface projects on public neural recordings; with no new labels, the cursor decoder averages R² 0.72 (1 is perfect) on 17 of 19 held-out days up to day 138, against 0.42 for a Wiener filter, the classic linear decoder.

2026 · toy maze, 3 seeds

Post Training Lab

A 59k-parameter transformer learns a maze by copying demonstrations (SFT), then two kinds of post-training, GRPO and DPO, lift held-out success from 70% to 80% and 81% over 3 seeds.

What didn’t work 150-level eval sets and 3 seeds, so one level is worth about 0.7 points.

2026 · simulation only; paper draft

Drone Autonomy

A simulated quadrotor that maps unknown rooms, plans with A* (a shortest-path search) and adapts to payload, wind and worn motors; a learned correction on top of the controller (a residual) roughly halves tuned adaptive control’s error on held-out tasks.

What didn’t work Out of distribution every method fails (navigation success ≤ 17%), and learned residuals crash more often than the plain controller, 43–49% against 34%.

2021 – 2025

Deep Learning Scientist · PhysicsAI

DARPA-funded · remote

Turned synthetic-data generation into a closed RL loop: agents (TD3, PPO, DDPG) set an image generator’s parameters, a spatiotemporal object detector retrained on what it produced, and the detector’s accuracy on real drone imagery came back as the reward.

Built the meta-RL codebase: a PEARL agent over TD3 that learns a probabilistic embedding of each generator task, so it adapts to a new one in a few tries. Ran its multi-week GPU training runs.

Also implemented domain adaptation from scratch to hold the detector’s accuracy with scarce real labels and mostly synthetic data (10–15 GB datasets), and built and benchmarked the GAN pipelines (DCGAN, InfoGAN, FineGAN) that produced its training images.

2023 – 2024

Deep RL Researcher · Hybrid Robotics Lab

UC Berkeley · Prof. Koushil Sreenath

Trained one RL policy that takes a Unitree Go1 robot dog through the whole task (walk to the ball, kick it at a target, stay balanced) in place of a separate policy per skill. On the real robot it hit the target about 60% of the time: it learned entirely in simulation, which can’t fully reproduce the ball’s friction on the ground, how it deforms when kicked, or sensor noise.

The policy is PPO, trained in IsaacGym on a three-stage reward: reach the ball, kick it, then wait to see whether it scores. A reward gradient, a stage flag and masked inputs (the ball and goal positions are zeroed until they matter) let one network move between stages smoothly, and CPG-based gait tracking kept its walking steady. Also prototyped RLPD, SAC that learns from prior data, to fine-tune the simulation-trained policy on real robot runs and narrow the gap to hardware.

2020 – 2022

Research Assistant · Video & Image Processing Lab

BAIR, UC Berkeley · Prof. Avideh Zakhor

Built a deep-learning pipeline that outlines invasive melanoma pixel by pixel in very large tissue images, to help pathologists measure how far a cancer has spread.

Invasive and in-situ melanoma look alike under the microscope; what matters is whether the tumour has crossed below the epidermis. So the pipeline segments the epidermis and the melanoma separately, at four magnifications, and keeps the melanoma outside the epidermis. Trained on slides from 57 UCSF patients outlined by two pathologists, it raised F1 on invasive melanoma to 0.44, from 0.14 for the previous best.

Published: SPIE Medical Imaging 2023 · USCAP 2022, Stowell-Orbison Award Poster Session

2020

Researcher · Abbasi-Asl Lab, UCSF

UC San Francisco · Prof. Reza Abbasi-Asl

Built pipelines that line up 2D and 3D brain scans so they can be compared, such as the same brain across visits or different brains against a common template. They rest on empirical mode decomposition, which splits an image into layers from fine detail down to a smooth residue, extended here from flat signals to full 3D volumes.

Before

  1. Look at the data before the model

During

2020

Defensive Iterative Retraining

Class report · CS285 Deep Reinforcement Learning, UC Berkeley · with Aman Shah and Srikar Boinapally

Training agents to resist adversarial policies by mixing past selves and freshly trained adversaries into the opponent pool, instead of plain self-play. In SumoAnts it slightly beat self-play; in the harder SumoHumans it fell behind, because the interruptions came before the agent had learned to stay on its feet.
