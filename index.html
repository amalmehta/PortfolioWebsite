DARPA-funded · remote

2023 – 2024

Deep RL Researcher · Hybrid Robotics Lab

UC Berkeley · Prof. Koushil Sreenath · a robot dog that learns to shoot a soccer ball

Trained one RL policy that takes a Unitree Go1 through the whole task (walk to the ball, kick it at a target, stay balanced) in place of a separate policy per skill. On the real robot it hit the target about 60% of the time: it learned entirely in simulation, which can’t fully reproduce the ball’s friction on the ground, how it deforms when kicked, or sensor noise. Took graduate classes in RL and control theory alongside.

The policy is PPO, trained in IsaacGym on a three-stage reward: reach the ball, kick it, then wait to see whether it scores. A reward gradient, a stage flag and masked inputs (the ball and goal positions are zeroed until they matter) let one network move between stages smoothly, and CPG-based gait tracking kept its walking steady. Also prototyped RLPD, SAC that learns from prior data, to fine-tune the simulation-trained policy on real robot runs and narrow the gap to hardware.

2020 – 2022

Research Assistant · Video & Image Processing Lab

BAIR, UC Berkeley · Prof. Avideh Zakhor

Built a deep-learning pipeline that outlines invasive melanoma pixel by pixel in very large tissue images, to help pathologists measure how far a cancer has spread.

Invasive and in-situ melanoma look alike under the microscope; what matters is whether the tumour has crossed below the epidermis. So the pipeline segments the epidermis and the melanoma separately, at four magnifications, and keeps the melanoma outside the epidermis. Trained on slides from 57 UCSF patients outlined by two pathologists, it raised F1 on invasive melanoma to 0.44, from 0.14 for the previous best.

Most of the work was engineering around the slides: cutting very large images into patches that fit on two GPUs, dropping empty ones, removing glue stains, and rebalancing rare classes with flips and rotations. HRNet+OCR started from Cityscapes weights and was benchmarked against HookNet, U-Net and the earlier published model.

Published: SPIE Medical Imaging 2023 · USCAP 2022, Stowell-Orbison Award Poster Session

Selected projects

Rewarding What You Can’t Verify

A research program on post-training long-horizon code agents when the checks you can run are narrower than correctness. In the pilot, an agent trained against visible tests alone learns to delete 98% of the code branches no test reaches: its pass rate climbs while real correctness falls from 31% to 2%. Auditing a random 25% of episodes with a hidden check prevents it. I then pre-registered 50 harder questions, each with a prediction and a kill criterion: 21 were supported, 15 refuted, 12 mixed and 2 deferred. It grew out of Reward Hack Zoo, five small post-training environments whose naive rewards a model can game, from length bias to self-grading.

BCI Projects

Four machine-learning projects for brain–computer interfaces, each with real public neural data where it exists, strong baselines, a closed-loop simulator and a web demo. Cursor decoding still scores R² 0.72 up to 138 days later with no new labels; attempted speech becomes text at a 38.8% phoneme error rate; assistance lifts a 7-joint robot arm’s simulated grasp success from 2% to 100%; and a learned visual-cortex stimulation encoder beats plain intensity-based stimulation. Three come with papers. A follow-up on the FALCON benchmark, Neuro Falcon, finds that re-scaling a decoder’s inputs with a few seconds of unlabelled new-day data beats fine-tuning on 20 seconds of labelled data.

Post Training Lab

A 59k-parameter transformer learns a key-and-door maze: supervised fine-tuning on demos from a planner with a bad habit, then two ways to fix it, GRPO (online RL on a verifiable reward) and DPO (offline preferences). Averaged over 3 seeds, GRPO lifts held-out success from 70% to 80% and DPO to 81%; RL from scratch on the same budget reaches 0%, and a run with a subtly wrong reward games it down to 7%.

A browser self-driving stack, from perception and prediction to planning and control, with a learned speed policy that can only act through a safety check. 2020

Defensive Iterative Retraining

CS285 Deep Reinforcement Learning, UC Berkeley · with Aman Shah and Srikar Boinapally

Training agents to resist adversarial policies by mixing past selves and freshly trained adversaries into the opponent pool, instead of plain self-play. In SumoAnts it slightly beat self-play; in the harder SumoHumans it fell behind, because the interruptions came before the agent had learned to stay on its feet.
